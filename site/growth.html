Contributions stop at the end of your chosen plan year. Withdrawals start in the first month of their chosen year and stay at the same dollar amount. If both are active, both happen. You can only take out money available in the account; unpaid withdrawals are counted as a shortfall. These examples help you compare stop dates, but cannot tell you a guaranteed safe year to stop saving.

How we build these examples

Real periods uses every available consecutive period of your chosen length in the 1928–2025 return data. Those periods overlap and are not independent. Mix years uses 5,000 repeatable random sequences, choosing unique years within each sequence. Mixing years removes the market’s original sequence and can understate prolonged bad markets.

The table groups ending balances around the 10th, 25th, 75th and 90th percentiles. Each example goes into exactly one group; tied amounts stay together. Percentages are counted from the examples, so they can differ from 10%, 15%, 50%, 15%, 10%. Empty groups are omitted. Amounts are rounded to whole dollars.

Annual returns are converted to an equivalent monthly rate. Contributions enter at the start of each month; dividends are reinvested. The chart shows annual points, not actual monthly market moves. These are nominal dollars before fees, taxes and inflation. Future results can fall outside every range shown. Understanding historical performance
